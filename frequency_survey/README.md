# QRUM Astrophysical Frequency Survey
## A Unified Statistical Analysis of Compact Object Frequency Distributions
**Author:** P. Gabor  **Version:** v2.1  **Status:** Scientific revision (2026)
This repository section accompanies the revised monograph:
**QRUM Astrophysical Frequency Survey: A Unified Statistical Analysis of Compact Object Frequency Distributions**
The study investigates frequency distributions across multiple classes of compact astrophysical objects using a common statistical methodology.
## Scope
The analysed populations include:
- pulsating white dwarfs,- pulsars,- magnetars,- high-frequency quasi-periodic oscillations (HFQPOs).
The analysis applies a unified statistical framework including:
- Kernel Density Estimation (KDE),- Gaussian Mixture Modelling (GMM),- Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) model selection,- bootstrap resampling,- Monte Carlo validation,- object-weighted statistics,- sensitivity and robustness analyses.
## Scientific interpretation
This work was originally developed within the broader **Quantum Resonance Unified Model (QRUM)** research programme.
The astrophysical frequency survey itself is presented as a statistically testable and independently reproducible investigation.
QRUM is retained as a possible interpretative framework and is **not assumed or claimed to be established by the statistical results presented here**.
Any proposed QRUM-related interpretation should therefore be treated separately from the empirical statistical findings and tested through independent observations and replication.

## Testable prediction
Within the QRUM interpretative framework, the observed statistical organization motivates a falsifiable prediction: if the proposed frequency-structured behaviour reflects an underlying physical regularity rather than a sample-specific statistical feature, comparable frequency organization should also emerge in additional compact-object populations analysed with the same methodology.
The prediction is therefore prospective and testable. Newly observed objects and independently constructed datasets can be used to determine whether the inferred frequency structure persists, shifts systematically between object classes, or disappears as the available samples increase.
Failure of future independent datasets to reproduce the predicted class-dependent frequency organization would count against this QRUM-based interpretation.
Accordingly, the prediction is kept conceptually separate from the empirical statistical results already obtained.
## Reproducibility
The purpose of this repository section is to support transparent and independent examination of the analysis.
The statistical methodology is intended to be applicable independently of the QRUM interpretation.
Independent replication, alternative statistical treatments, and critical examination of the reported frequency structure are encouraged.
